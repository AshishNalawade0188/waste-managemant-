Technology — Catalytic Thermal Decomposition

How it works

Plastic feedstock is heated in an oxygen-limited (anaerobic) reactor. In presence of a catalyst and staged heating zones, long polymer chains break into shorter hydrocarbons. Vapors are condensed into liquid fractions (diesel-like), non-condensable gases (syngas) are recovered, and inert char remains as solid residue. Continuous monitoring of temperature and pressure ensures safe, optimized operation.

Reaction Chamber Design

  • Insulated, staged zones for controlled cracking.
  • Feed system with metering to avoid oxygen ingress.
  • Condensation train for fraction separation (light/medium/heavy).
  • Integrated scrubber and catalytic converter for emissions control.

Types of Plastics

Suitable: PE, PP, PS and most mixed thermoplastics. Caution: PVC and chlorine-containing plastics require pre-treatment due to HCl formation — avoid without professional chemical scrubbing.

Outputs & Safety

Liquid fuel (density approx. 0.85 kg/L), syngas (combustible), and char. Safety systems: temperature/pressure interlocks, gas detectors, emergency shutdown, continuous emissions monitoring. Real deployment requires permits and expert engineering.

Waste Conversion Process (Collapsible)

  1. Feed preparation & sorting: remove metals, glass, PVC.
  2. Pre-treatment: shredding & drying for consistent feed.
  3. Reactor heating: oxygen-limited, staged zones.
  4. Catalytic decomposition: catalyst accelerates chain scission at lower temps.
  5. Condensation & fractionation: separate light/medium/heavy fractions.
  6. Gas cleaning: scrubbers and filters for syngas.
  7. Residue: char handling and potential reuse in materials.
  8. Emissions control: scrubbers, catalytic converters, monitoring.

Safety note: This demo provides conceptual information. Real installations require licensed engineers, environmental permits, and emissions testing.
